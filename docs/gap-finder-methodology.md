# Gap Finder — role logic and evidence boundaries

**Review date:** 22 November 2026  
**Squad snapshot:** RR's official 18 November 2026 retention / release announcement and the 19-player squad dataset in this repository.

The Gap Finder is a franchise-role assessment, not a general ranking of the best cricketers. A gap survives only when the candidate adds a distinct role, improves a plausible XI, or closes a practical absence-coverage scenario. It must be assessed against both the retained squad and the limits of the evidence.

## 1. Pace depth: insurance, not a vacancy

### What RR already has

The retained pace group is not a blank slate. The dashboard now uses this role map:

- **Mohammed Shami and Nandre Burger:** preferred new-ball / powerplay profiles (right-arm and left-arm options).
- **Jofra Archer:** the premium strike bowler in this group, with new-ball quality and credible death-over flexibility.
- **Sandeep Sharma:** control, variations and experienced phase cover.
- **Brijesh Sharma:** Indian pace depth with a retained death-over option.
- **Sushant Mishra:** developmental left-arm reserve / rotation depth.

Boult is a historical RR new-ball / powerplay reference. His departure should not be used to imply that RR currently has no new-ball options, nor should his role be confused with a death-over role.

### What the playoff evidence can and cannot say

The 2022 Qualifier 1 and 2026 Qualifier 2 chases ended with the opposition losing only three wickets. They support a question about controlling set partnerships and creating follow-up wicket pressure. They do not prove that pace bowling alone caused either result, that RR has no death options, or that one recruit would have changed either match.

### Recruitment implication

A further Indian quick is an **advantage / insurance purchase**, not an urgent headcount correction. The case is workload and injury resilience: add a player only if he offers a distinct multi-phase skill, improves dependable overs 17–20 options, or makes the retained group more robust when one or more frontline bowlers are unavailable.

Avesh Khan and Akash Madhwal should be compared against the existing role portfolio, not presented as automatic fixes for an empty slot. Avesh's supplied death-phase record (31 wickets from 460 legal balls; 6.74 wickets per 100) is below the retained-core median of 8.12, so he is a depth / rotation case rather than a proven first-choice closer. Akash remains conditional because the supplied phase sample is smaller and conflicting.

The retained group should not be described as having no death-over cover: Archer, Sandeep and Brijesh have different forms of phase utility. Tushar Deshpande is not elevated simply because he is a released former RR player; any return would need to beat Brijesh/Sandeep on role differentiation and run-cost, not just point to his historical wicket count.

## 1A. Hasaranga considered, not prioritised

Wanindu Hasaranga is a plausible role-profile alternative: attacking leg-spin paired with potential lower-order batting / hitting utility. That two-skill package makes him worth acknowledging in the decision audit; it is not proof of dependable IPL finishing output. It does not create a clean vacancy, because RR already have wrist-spin through Ravi Bishnoi and Yash Raj Punja, plus left-arm orthodox spin through Ravindra Jadeja and Manimaran Siddharth.

The official 18 November 2026 squad snapshot shows five overseas players retained and three overseas squad places remaining under the eight-player squad cap. Hasaranga would use one of those three places, so the decision must be compared against overseas batting and pace alternatives.

The availability caution is based on reported interruptions, not a diagnosis of current fitness: ICC reported a left-hamstring injury in August 2024 and a recurring left-hamstring issue in November 2024; he missed IPL 2024 with a heel injury; Daily FT reported another hamstring concern during the 2026 T20 World Cup and a previous hamstring operation in 2023. Reconsideration requires current independent medical clearance and a clear XI upgrade after slot cost.

**Decision:** Hasaranga is a considered alternative, not a base-plan recommendation. Revisit only if the final registered auction pool, medical assessment and resulting XI justify using an overseas slot on him.

## 2. Middle-order role succession after Hetmyer's release

RR's official 2027 retention announcement confirms Shimron Hetmyer was released. His departure removes an overseas left-hand No.6 / finishing option from the previous group. This is a **role-succession question**, not a claim that Hetmyer was consistently reliable or that RR must buy an overseas like-for-like replacement.

The retained group still has meaningful cover:

- Riyan Parag and Dhruv Jurel form the middle-order spine.
- Ravindra Jadeja adds batting depth and an all-round option.
- Shubham Dubey is a retained left-hand middle/lower-order hitter.
- Donovan Ferreira and Dasun Shanaka provide overseas lower-order / all-round options.

Those players cover components of the role, but the analyst should test whether the preferred XI has a dependable No.5/6 option who can enter early after top-order wickets, repair an innings, handle spin and still accelerate. This is different from buying a generic last-five-overs finisher.

The historical RR examples of Rahul Tewatia and Chris Morris are useful only as **role precedents**: Tewatia was used as lower-order utility / a batting all-round option, while Morris was recruited as a pace-bowling all-rounder with death-bowling value. They are not direct Hetmyer replacements and should not be used to prescribe a specific player type without checking the current XI.

The two detailed playoff batting cases—the 2022 Final and 2024 Qualifier 2—show four and five RR wickets respectively in overs 7–15. Their 49-run figures are batter-run components in the supplied export, not official innings phase totals because extras are categorically encoded. These are case studies, not a five-season proof of a universal middle-order weakness.

Nehal Wadhera and Nitish Rana remain comparison profiles for the already-submitted target board. This revision does not alter the target list or its submitted review order.

## 3. Squad absence stress test — not playoff evidence

This is a separate selector stress test. It is **not** one of the playoff findings and must not be displayed under a heading such as “What the playoffs showed.” The rule-based XI engine was run once for each of the 19 retained players, removing that player from the available pool and checking whether it could construct an XI that passed the current role tags and selection constraints.

- **16 scenarios:** the current engine found a role/constraint-valid XI.
- **3 scenarios:** the current engine did not find one — Dhruv Jurel, Vaibhav Sooryavanshi and Yashasvi Jaiswal.

These are counts from a fixed software stress test, not a percentage accuracy score. There is no labelled historical test set of actual absences and observed replacement performance against which the selector has been calibrated. A coach may identify a practical alternative that the current role tags or constraints miss; conversely, a rule-valid XI is not proof of equivalent performance. The three failed cases are prompts to review the engine's role definitions and the cricket practicality of the available combinations, not proof that RR has no cover.

## 4. Decision discipline

1. Do not equate squad headcount with specialist cover.
2. Do not equate a player's presence in the candidate pool with registration, medical clearance or availability.
3. Do not infer a causal gap from two or three playoff matches.
4. Do not interpret role tags as verified bowling workload or like-for-like performance.
5. Keep candidate names and their submitted review order intact; this pass improves the explanation of the squad problem, not the submitted shortlist.
